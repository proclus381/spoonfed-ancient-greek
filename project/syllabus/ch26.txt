# Smyth inventory (added 2026-11-23): the uses of the cases. The book's summary in this chapter lists the adverbial accusative, the genitive of separation, with verbs and with adjectives, the dative of interest, verbs with the dative and the dative “with”; the others are Smyth
X:acc-adv | the adverbial accusative: neuter adjectives and pronouns as adverbs (μέγα βοᾷ “shouts loudly”, οὐδέν “not at all”, τὸ πρῶτον “at first”, τὰ πολλά “for the most part”); Smyth §1606–1611
X:acc-cognate | the cognate (internal) accusative: a noun of related meaning as object (μάχην μάχεσθαι “fight a battle”, νίκην νικᾶν “win a victory”, βίον ζῆν “live a life”); Smyth §1563–1567
X:gen-sep | the genitive of separation and origin: παύομαι τοῦ ἔργου “I stop work”, ἀπέχει τῆς θαλάττης “is far from the sea”, ἀγαθοῦ πατρὸς γενόμενος “born of a good father”; Smyth §1392, §1410
X:gen-verbs | verbs with the genitive: taking hold (λαμβάνομαι), ruling (ἄρχω, κρατέω, βασιλεύω), hitting and missing (τυγχάνω, ἁμαρτάνω), perceiving and touching (ἀκούω, αἰσθάνομαι, ἅπτομαι), sharing (μετέχω); Smyth §1341–1370
X:gen-adj | the genitive with adjectives: ἄξιος τιμῆς “worthy of honour”, αἴτιος τοῦ πολέμου “responsible for the war”, μεστός, ἔμπειρος, ἄπειρος + gen.; Smyth §1412
X:gen-cause | the genitive of cause with verbs of feeling: θαυμάζω σε τῆς ἀνδρείας “I admire you for your courage”, οἰκτίρω σε τῆς συμφορᾶς “I pity you for your misfortune”; Smyth §1405
X:gen-excl | the genitive in exclamations, often after an interjection: φεῦ τοῦ ἀνδρός “alas for the man!”; Smyth §1407
X:dat-interest | the dative of interest (advantage or disadvantage): “for” (τοῖς παισὶν οἰκίαν ᾠκοδόμησεν “he built a house for his children”); Smyth §1481
X:dat-verbs | verbs with the dative where English has a direct object: ἕπομαι, ἀκολουθέω “follow”, βοηθέω “help”, πείθομαι “obey”, χράομαι “use”, μάχομαι “fight”; Smyth §1460
X:dat-accomp | the dative of accompaniment: with αὐτός “… and all” (αὐτοῖς τοῖς ναύταις “crews and all”), and in military movements (πολλῷ στρατῷ “with a large army”); Smyth §1524–1526
X:dat-manner | the dative of manner: σιγῇ “in silence”, βίᾳ “by force”, δρόμῳ “at a run”, σπουδῇ “in haste”, τούτῳ τῷ τρόπῳ “in this way”; Smyth §1513, §1527
X:dat-cause | the dative of cause: “from, through, out of” (φόβῳ “from fear”, ὕβρει “out of insolence”, ἀγνοίᾳ “through ignorance”); Smyth §1517
X:dat-ethic | the ethical dative: μοι, ἡμῖν showing the speaker's concern, often “please” (μή μοι θορυβεῖτε “please don't make a noise”); Smyth §1486
X:gen-measure | the genitive of measure and material: ὁδὸς τριῶν ἡμερῶν “a three days' journey”, τεῖχος ὀκτὼ σταδίων “a wall eight stades long”; Smyth §1323, §1325
